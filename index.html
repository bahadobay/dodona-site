Duy — "hear" in Turkish

Ask your audio anything.

Duy listens to a recording and answers your questions about it — how it was said, who was talking, what was said — each with a probability you can trust.

  • a 10-second support call · illustrative example
  • Does the caller sound angry?84%
  • Are they talking fast?71%
  • Do two people talk over each other?12%
  • Did they say "cancel"?96%
now → training the release model

What makes it different

One pass, no text to parse The usual way to question an audio language model is to let it write an answer, token by token, for each question, then parse the text. Duy reads the probability of every answer option straight from the model, for all questions at once: about 40 questions on a 10-second clip in 0.36 s on one GPU.
Probabilities that mean what they say On the questions it was trained for, when Duy says 85% it is right about 85% of the time on held-out recordings. The model card will show the full reliability tables, not just one summary number.
Emotion, delivery and content together One model hears how someone feels, how they speak (pace, hesitation, overlap, number of speakers) and what they said — so you can ask about all of them in the same pass.

Ears for Claude

Duy is being built with an MCP connector for Claude. Claude hands Duy a recording and its questions, gets calibrated answers back, and reasons over them: reviewing hundreds of support calls for frustration, summarising who dominated a meeting, or flagging the recordings a person should listen to. Claude does the reasoning; Duy gives it ears it can trust.

Status

  • In development. Experiments are pre-registered and run on AWS.
  • Built on Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct with a small adapter, trained on English speech: AMI meetings, CREMA-D, Common Voice.
  • Release: open source (Apache-2.0) on Hugging Face, with a model card that states what it can and cannot answer.
  • Not a chat model: Duy answers questions about audio; it does not hold a conversation.
