Dodona

the oracle that listened

Ask your audio anything.

Dodona listens to a recording and answers your questions about it: how it was said, who was talking and what was said. Each answer comes with a probability you can trust.

  • a 10-second support call · illustrative example
  • Each percentage is how likely that answer is, not a share of the recording.
  • yes / no Did they say "cancel"? Yes 96%
  • one of three · how likely each option is Which emotion comes through most? angry71% neutral22% sad7%

What makes it different

One pass, no text to parse The usual way to question an audio language model is to let it write an answer, token by token, for each question, then parse the text. Dodona reads the probability of every answer option straight from the model, for all questions at once: about 40 questions on a 10-second clip in 0.36 s on one GPU.
Probabilities that mean what they say On the questions it was trained for, when Dodona says 85% it is right about 85% of the time on held-out recordings. The model card will show the full reliability tables, not just one summary number.
Emotion, delivery and content together One model hears how someone feels, how they speak (pace, hesitation, overlap, number of speakers) and what they said, so you can ask about all of them in the same pass.

Ears for Claude

Dodona is being built with an MCP connector for Claude. Claude hands Dodona a recording and its questions, gets calibrated answers back, and reasons over them: reviewing hundreds of support calls for frustration, summarising who dominated a meeting, or flagging the recordings a person should listen to. Claude does the reasoning; Dodona gives it ears it can trust.

Why Dodona

Dodona was the oldest oracle of ancient Greece. Its priests heard the answers in sound: the rustling of a sacred oak, the calls of doves, the ringing of bronze cauldrons in the wind. People wrote their questions on small lead tablets, most of them yes-or-no ("is it better for me to…?"), and thousands survive. A written question, a recording to listen to, a yes or a no: that is still the whole idea.

Status

  • In development. Experiments are pre-registered and run on AWS.
  • Built on Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct with a small adapter, trained on English speech: AMI meetings, CREMA-D, Common Voice.
  • Release: open source (Apache-2.0) on Hugging Face, with a model card that states what it can and cannot answer.
  • Not a chat model: Dodona answers questions about audio; it does not hold a conversation.
